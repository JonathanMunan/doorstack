Why invest in Calgary

Canada's fastest-growing big city. Still one of its most affordable.

Calgary pairs strong population growth with some of the lowest housing costs of any major Canadian city, a tax-friendly province and a lifestyle that keeps people moving here. Here is a quick look at the city, the numbers and where the market sits today.

Growth

People keep choosing Calgary.

Between 2021 and 2025 the Calgary metro area added nearly 296,000 residents, growing faster than any other large Canadian city while Toronto's population barely moved.

Growth is now easing as federal immigration targets come down. The City of Calgary expects about 34,000 new residents in 2026, after gains of 53,000 and 88,000 in the two years before. That is still a city adding the population of a small town every year, and every one of those people needs somewhere to live.

Newcomers arrive from across Canada and around the world, drawn by jobs in energy, technology, construction, logistics and business services, plus housing costs that remain within reach.

The Alberta advantage

Lower costs to buy, own and operate.

No provincial sales tax

Alberta is the only province with no provincial sales tax, which keeps everyday costs and many operating expenses lower.

No land transfer tax

Buyers pay modest land title registration fees instead of the land transfer taxes charged in Ontario and British Columbia. On a multi-unit purchase, that difference adds up.

No rent control

Alberta has no provincial rent control on market rentals. Landlords follow the Residential Tenancies Act for notice and timing of increases.

High household incomes

Calgary incomes are among the highest in the country, so tenants can comfortably afford a quality rental without stretching.

Life in Calgary

A city people move to, and stay in.

Ranked for livability

Calgary placed fifth in the world and first in North America on the Economist Intelligence Unit's 2024 Global Liveability Index, with perfect scores for stability, healthcare and education.

The mountains next door

The Rockies are close enough for a day of hiking, skiing or snowboarding, a major draw for families and young professionals.

Sunny, friendly and family-first

Strong schools, low crime and a welcoming, entrepreneurial culture help new arrivals settle in fast.

A connected city

An international airport, a CTrain network and major highway links to the rest of Alberta and B.C.

Where the market sits today

Communities to know

Where renters want to live.

Walkable inner-city neighbourhoods and transit-connected areas continue to outperform. A few worth knowing:

Beltline

Calgary's largest rental area, steps from downtown, restaurants and the Stampede grounds. Popular with young professionals.

Bridgeland

A trendy inner-city village with river pathways, local cafés and quick CTrain access to downtown.

Mission and Marda Loop

Walkable streets lined with shops and patios in the southwest, where vacancy has run lowest and rents highest.

University District

A master-planned, walkable community beside the University of Calgary and major hospitals, with steady demand from students and healthcare workers.

Seton

A fast-growing southeast hub built around the South Health Campus, with retail, recreation and new condo supply.

Surrounding communities

Airdrie, Cochrane and Okotoks offer lower entry prices and growing commuter populations within easy reach of the city.
